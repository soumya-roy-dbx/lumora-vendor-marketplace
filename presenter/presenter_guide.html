07Questions they're likely to ask

Grouped the way they tend to come: the domain, the business value, what we're proposing, and the impact. Click to expand.

The domain
Why is third-party data such a big deal for a CRO?

CROs run trials and commercial programs for sponsors. Picking countries and sites (feasibility), finding investigators and HCPs, sizing patient populations and building real-world evidence all depend on licensed data from specialist vendors — HCP/HCO master data, claims, EHR-derived patient data, trial registries, publications. Choosing the wrong vendor, or not knowing one already covers a country, costs time at study start and money in duplicate licences.

What do HCP, HCO, RWD mean here?

HCP = healthcare professional (physicians, nurses, pharmacists — the people you recruit as investigators or target commercially). HCO = healthcare organization (hospitals, clinics, sites). RWD = real-world data from routine care (claims, EHR, registries), used to generate real-world evidence.

Why Egypt as the example?

Emerging markets are where vendor coverage is thinnest and least obvious — exactly where a spreadsheet hunt fails and a complete answer matters. In the demo only 3 of 18 vendors cover HCP data in Egypt; the old pattern found one. The same query works for any country, data type or therapeutic area.

Is any of this real data? Does vendor data contain patient information?

Everything shown is synthetic — a fictional CRO and fictional vendors. In production, HCP data is professional personal data (names, emails), and patient-level data from vendors is typically de-identified. Either way the pattern is the same: classify sensitive columns and apply masks/row filters in Unity Catalog, plus the AI Gateway PII guardrail at the prompt boundary. Whether any of their vendor data is PHI is a question for their data/privacy team.

Our vendors send catalogs in different formats. Does that break this?

No — that's what the Bronze/Silver layers are for: land as-is, then standardize into one model with quality expectations. Auto Loader handles new and changed files incrementally, and failing records are measured rather than silently dropped. Real-world catalogs need a mapping step per format; that's part of the first 30 days.

Business value
Where does the ~$600K come from? Is it real?

It's an illustrative model with the formulas on Slide 9: analyst time saved ($313.5K), duplicate purchases avoided ($180K), renewal savings ($108K). The inputs are assumptions, clearly labelled. The first pilot deliverable is to replace them with Lumora's request volumes, analyst cost and data spend. Even with every lever halved it's ~$222K against an assumed $50K run cost.

What KPIs would we track to prove it?

Request turnaround (days → minutes), answer completeness vs. a gold set of real questions, analyst hours per request, duplicate-licence findings, renewals reviewed before notice deadline, spend at risk, data-quality pass rates, and AI evaluation scores per release.

What's the payback period?

On the illustrative model, under two months; on the conservative case, still within the first quarter of use. We'd confirm with a sizing of their actual workload — serverless, scales to zero, so cost tracks usage.

We already have a chatbot that answers vendor questions. Why change?

Because the hard part isn't the chat — it's completeness and trust. Pasting spreadsheets into a prompt gives answers that vary and can't be audited. Here, the facts come from governed tables with SQL shown, documents come from a separate assistant with citations, access is enforced per user, and every release is evaluated. You can keep the same front end.

What we're proposing
What exactly are you proposing we do?

A 30-day pilot: land 3–5 real vendor catalogs into governed tables, stand up Genie on them, and measure turnaround and completeness on 20 of your real questions against today. If it hits the KPIs, phases 2 and 3 add governance, the document assistant, the evaluation gate, the dashboard and the app (Slide 11).

What do you need from our team?

An executive sponsor and a named domain owner, 3–5 vendor catalogs, 20 real sourcing questions, a Databricks workspace (or access to yours), and confirmation of the value-model inputs. We bring the reference build, packaged as a Databricks Asset Bundle so it deploys as code.

Do we have to rebuild our current app?

No. The marketplace UI stays familiar; what changes is the engine under it — Lakebase-served tiles and an assistant wired to the governed agents. The app can also embed the dashboard later.

Why one Genie space and an agent — isn't Genie enough?

Genie is the right tool for structured questions (and starts the pilot). Some questions live in documents — methodology, contract terms, onboarding — so a supervisor routes those to a Knowledge Assistant. Start simple; add the supervisor when document questions matter.

Who maintains it after go-live?

The domain owner owns the catalog content: column descriptions, metric definitions and data-quality rules (in plain English). Changes live in Git and deploy through bundles, so they're reviewed like code. Platform operations are serverless.

How it impacts us
How does this change a sourcing analyst's day?

Instead of searching spreadsheets and emailing vendor managers, they ask the question, get every qualifying vendor with the SQL, and spend their time on the decision. Requests that took days are answered in the same session.

How does it change risk and compliance?

Contract cost and vendor contact details are visible only to the roles that need them — enforced in one place for every tool. Prompts with personal data are blocked at the gateway. Every answer is traceable to SQL or a cited document, and access is auditable through Unity Catalog's audit logs.

What happens to our existing spreadsheets and SharePoint?

They become the input, not the system of record. Vendor managers keep dropping exports where they do today (or into a governed volume); the pipeline picks them up incrementally. Over time the governed tables become the single source of truth.

What could go wrong?

Messy source catalogs (handled by the Silver layer and DQ rules), unclear ownership (hence a named domain owner), and AI answers drifting as data changes — which is why the evaluation derives its expected answers from the governed tables and runs every release. We saw that gate catch a real issue in this build.

Quick technical follow-ups
Does Genie respect the masks?

Yes — it runs SQL as the person asking. You saw it: finance sees $400,000; the app's identity sees it masked.

Do masks carry into Lakebase?

No — Lakebase is a separate Postgres serving store. The pattern is to publish only what the app needs; our vendor_tiles has no cost column by design.

Can Power BI or Tableau use the metric views?

Anything that runs SQL on the warehouse can query them with MEASURE(). Native semantic integration with external BI tools is evolving — we'll confirm current support before committing.
